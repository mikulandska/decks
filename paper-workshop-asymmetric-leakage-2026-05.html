M. VILJANEN01 / 09TITLE
Open with the central question. AIA and CSDDD are both metaregulatory. Both must eventually meet law's binary verification machinery. What gives way? That is what the paper is about. Skip pleasantries. Be on slide 2 within 45 seconds.
260526/AIS/HAM

Two 2024 instruments, one apparent template

  • AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689). Providers of high-risk AI systems must build risk management systems calibrated to EU fundamental rights.
  • CSDDD (Directive 2024/1760). Large companies must build due diligence processes covering human rights and environment in their value chains.
  • Both metaregulatory. The regulator does not prescribe conduct. It mandates that the firm build an internal cognitive architecture that senses, plans, and acts.
  • Both put rights at the architectural centre, not the periphery.
  • The labels suggest family resemblance. Management-based regulation. Due diligence. Risk management.
M. VILJANEN02 / 09SETUP
Quick setup. The audience knows these instruments exist. The convergence story is on the surface. The deeper question is whether the surface holds. Spend no more than 60 seconds here.
260526/AIS/HAM

Two cognitive logics. One legal logic.

Both instruments produce cognitive output that eventually must surface as something law can verify.

Law's enforcement outputs are binary. Compliant or not. Violated or not. Even when reasoning is gradated, dispositive outputs are binary.

But AIA cognition is gradient. Severity and probability scores. Continuous optimisation toward an undefined target.

And CSDDD cognition is state-oriented. Adverse impact present or absent. Rights violated or not.

The collision point differs by orientation. What gives way?

M. VILJANEN04 / 09FRAMEWORK
Spend 90 seconds maximum. Sensing modality is the form of output the regime requires. Binary state, or scalar gradient. Telos determinacy is whether the substantive endpoint is fixed. AIA optimises toward an undefined horizon. CSDDD enforces a floor. The two empty quadrants exist in the paper but are off-scope for this talk.
260526/AIS/HAM

AIA: gradient cognition meets binary verification

Article 9 mandates a continuous risk management cycle through the system's lifecycle. The output is scalar.

Article 9(5): residual risk must be "judged to be acceptable." The statute does not specify who judges or by what standard. The boundary is the firm's.

Article 79 / MSR Article 3(19): the enforcer intervenes when the system "presents a risk." A different standard from Article 9(5).

The standard-mismatch is structural. The firm's acceptability judgment is not what the enforcer reviews. Substantive optimisation runs inside a wrapper. Doctrinal closure on Article 9(5) does not arrive.

Edelman's legal endogeneity as the parallel. The compliance architecture acquires substantive status by legislative design ex ante, not by judicial deference ex post.

M. VILJANEN05 / 09AIA
The punchline for the AIA side. The wrapper is the absorption mechanism. Gradient cognition produces an Article 9(5) judgment, which is binary in form (acceptable or not) but with no determinate boundary. The enforcer reviews a different question entirely. Optimisation stays insulated. Edelman link is one line on the slide and one sentence in delivery. Do not unpack her work unless asked.
260526/AIS/HAM

CSDDD: state-oriented cognition meets determinate enforcement

Articles 8, 10, 11. The firm must identify, prevent, and end adverse impacts. Objects are binary, occasionally ternary (potential, actual, absent).

Article 3(q) defines "appropriate measures." The same standard governs both the firm's planning and the enforcer's review. No mismatch.

The collision is absorbed without a wrapper. The cognition is already in law's preferred shape.

But: when remediation cannot be immediate, Article 10(2) requires action plans with timelines and indicators. Article 11 requires monitoring and revision.

The temporal cycle introduces gradient logic into a binary regime. "Are our prevention efforts working?" is a gradient question. The leakage is operational, not structural.

M. VILJANEN06 / 09CSDDD
CSDDD has no structural conflict with law because its cognition is already binary-compatible. The leakage toward gradient happens through the temporal cycle, not through enforcement architecture. Post-Omnibus deletion of civil liability slows doctrinal closure on Article 3(q) but does not change its direction. This becomes important for the implications slide.
260526/AIS/HAM

Same machinery, opposite leaks

AIA · gradient → determinate

Structural pull. Law's binary enforcement machinery demands a verifiable output.

The wrapper produces one. Substantive optimisation stays insulated.

The leak is the price of admitting gradient cognition into law's binary frame.

CSDDD · state-oriented → gradient

Operational push. Value chain complexity and the impossibility of immediate remediation introduce temporal loops.

The loops carry gradient logic, but the endpoint stays binary.

The leak is the price of operating in time.

M. VILJANEN07 / 09ASYMMETRY
The asymmetric leakage punchline. Two architectures, opposite leak directions, different causes. AIA leakage is law-induced and structural. CSDDD leakage is reality-induced and operational. The asymmetry is the main analytical contribution to flag for Coaseman.
260526/AIS/HAM

What the Omnibus did

Directive (EU) 2026/470, in force 18 March 2026, deleted the harmonised CSDDD civil liability regime and raised scope thresholds to 5,000 employees and EUR 1.5 billion turnover.

For AIA. Nothing changed. The wrapper remains. The standard-mismatch deepens because no parallel enforcement architecture emerged to close it.

For CSDDD. Closure on Article 3(q) is now slower. No damages litigation channel. Supervisory enforcement appeals only. The substantive trajectory is preserved. The velocity drops.

The asymmetry deepens. AIA stays insulated. CSDDD doctrinal closure crawls.

M. VILJANEN08 / 09POST-OMNIBUS
Quick implications slide. Audience will likely want to discuss the Omnibus. Note that the analytical framework holds regardless of these changes. Only leakage velocities shift. If asked, the substantive standard does not change because Article 3(q) is preserved.
260526/AIS/HAM

Three questions for the commentator

The paper does not offer a positive theory of firm behaviour, a welfare comparison between architectures, or empirical work on whether the wrapper enables gaming or efficiency.

  1. Can the framework (sensing modality + telos determinacy) be derived from primitives — information costs, monitoring costs, political constraints?
  2. Is the AIA wrapper a design feature (firms hold superior information about acceptable risk) or a design flaw (industry capture)?
  3. What welfare comparison can be made between gradient + indeterminate (AIA) and state-oriented + determinate (CSDDD) architectures, holding the underlying rights constant?

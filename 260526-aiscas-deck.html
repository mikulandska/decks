1. Two 2024 instruments, one template

AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689). Providers of high-risk AI systems must build risk management systems calibrated to EU fundamental rights.

CSDDD (Directive 2024/1760). Large companies must build due diligence processes covering human and environmental rights in their value chains.

Both are metaregulatory. The regulator does not prescribe conduct. It mandates that the firm build an internal cognitive architecture that senses, plans, and acts.

Both put rights at the architectural centre, introduce a horizontal metaeffect for fundamental and human rights.

Quick setup. The audience knows these instruments exist. The convergence story is on the surface. The deeper question is whether the surface holds. Spend no more than 60 seconds here.

2. Two dimensions, four extremes

Despite similarities the two instruments differ in how they deploy rights to govern intra-corporation processes.

The paper treats firms as distributed cognitive systems. Regulation reshapes how they sense as rights and how they respond.

Sensing: binary / gradient rights as sensing targets.

Acting: determinate telos / indeterminate telos for action.

This is the framing of the paper. Management-based regulation generates cognitive outputs. Law demands binary verification. The two cannot stay in their pure form. The interesting analytical question is how the absorption happens. The answer will be different for each instrument, and the difference is structural.

4. AIA: gradient sensing with indeterminate aspirations

AIA Art 9 sensing is gradient: firms sense risks as a spectral entity.

AIA Art 9 action is indeterminately aspirational: Optimize until spectral residual risk "is judged acceptable" (by the firm).

AIA Arts 79 and 82 create an extremal fallback floor: system will be withdrawn if they have the potential to affect adversely fundamental rights "to a degree which goes beyond that considered reasonable and acceptable".

A normative wrapper: optimize to a non-reviewable spectral state above an extremal floor.

The punchline for the AIA side. The wrapper is the absorption mechanism. Gradient cognition produces an Article 9(5) judgment, which is binary in form (acceptable or not) but with no determinate boundary. The enforcer reviews a different question entirely. Optimisation stays insulated. Edelman link is one line on the slide and one sentence in delivery. Do not unpack her work unless asked.

6. CSDDD: state-oriented sensing with determinate binary action

CSDDD Art 8 sensing is ternary: firms take "appropriate measures" to sense existing, potential and absent adverse effects to rights.

CSDDD Arts 10 and 11 action is primarily determinate-binary: take "appropriate measures" to prevent or mitigate potential adverse impacts and bring to an end actual ones. Mitigation and bringing to an end timelines and indicators introduce gradient floor-seeking.

CSDDD Article 3(q) "appropriate measures " locks-in binary floor-seeking.

CSDDD has no structural conflict with law because its cognition is already binary-compatible. The leakage toward gradient happens through the temporal cycle, not through enforcement architecture. Post-Omnibus deletion of civil liability slows doctrinal closure on Article 3(q) but does not change its direction. This becomes important for the implications slide.

8. Both regimes leak to opposite quadrants

AIA

Indeterminate-aspirational action leaks to a determinate process-binary.

Wrapper insulation: Firms must demonstrate processual compliance, while the process outcomes remain in a wrapper beyond enforcement logic's reach.

CSDDD

Temporal softening: State-oriented sensing and binary outcome control leaks to gradient sensing and floor-seeking action in temporal loops when firms gradually mitigate and bring to an end adverse impacts.

The asymmetric leakage punchline. Two architectures, opposite leak directions, different causes. AIA leakage is law-induced and structural. CSDDD leakage is reality-induced and operational. The asymmetry is the main analytical contribution to flag for the commentator.

9. What the Omnibuses did

Digital Omnibus on AI (provisional agreement 7 May 2026). Annex III high-risk deadlines pushed to December 2027. Annex I to August 2028. "Safety component" narrowed to exclude assistive and optimising AI.

CSDDD Omnibus (Dir. 2026/470, in force 18 March 2026). Civil liability deleted. Thresholds raised to 5,000 employees and EUR 1.5 billion turnover.

For CSDDD. Enforcement channel narrowed. Doctrinal closure on Article 3(q) crawls.

For AIA. Delay and a narrower trigger. The wrapper has more time to consolidate. Fewer systems enter its scope.

Both regimes softer than originally planned. The asymmetric leakage thesis holds. Velocities slow on both sides.

Two omnibuses reshaped the post-2024 landscape. CSDDD Omnibus in March deleted civil liability and raised scope thresholds. Digital Omnibus on AI in May pushed deadlines and narrowed the safety-component definition. The earlier framing that AIA was untouched by Omnibus is wrong. Both regimes are now softer than planned, but the asymmetric leakage thesis survives. The leakage just runs slower in both directions. If asked about timing of the Digital Omnibus, the agreement is provisional. Formal adoption expected June or July 2026.

10. Why this setup?

AIA: aspirational ethics for the single market, but the wrapper rigs the package to fail down.

CSDDD: minimal ethics for global production. "Appropriate measures" keeps high ambition possible.

Both: activist rhetoric for politics, ineffective and loose structures for the firms.

Closing political-economy reading. The asymmetry has a logic, not just a structure. EU wanted to look ambitious on AI for the single market while preserving firm discretion under the wrapper. EU wanted minimum compliance on supply chains with room for the standard to grow through interpretation. Third corner names the common political-economy logic. Both regimes pair activist rhetoric (for the political audience) with structures that fail to bite for firms. The asymmetry has a pattern. Deliver this calmly. Do not over-claim political intent.
